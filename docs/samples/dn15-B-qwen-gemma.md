# 2.1 `Paṭiccasamuppāda` (dependent origination)

## Mahānidānasutta

## `Paṭiccasamuppāda` (dependent origination)

95. Thus I have heard— on one occasion the Bhagavā dwells in the Kurus at Kammāsadhamma, a town of the Kurus. Then indeed, the venerable Ānanda went to where the Bhagavā was; having gone there, having bowed down to the Bhagavā, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, indeed, the venerable Ānanda said this to the Bhagavā: “Wonderful, Bhante, extraordinary, Bhante. As long as profound and this, Bhante, `paṭiccasamuppādo` (dependent origination) is profound and appearing profound, but indeed to me very clear it appears.” “Do not say thus, Ānanda, do not say thus, Ānanda. Profound and this, Ānanda, `paṭiccasamuppādo` is profound and appearing profound. By the non-comprehension of this `dhamma` (teaching), Ānanda, by non-penetration, thus this people, like a tangled ball of thread, tangled like a bird's nest, like muñja grass and reeds, the state of loss, the bad destination, the lower realm, the `saṃsāra` (cycle of rebirth) does not go beyond.

96. “Is there `jarāmaraṇa` (old age and death) due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said. If he would say, “Due to what condition is old age and death?”, “Due to the condition of `jāti` (birth) is old age and death” thus to him should be said.

“Is there birth due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said to him. If he would say, “Due to what condition is birth?”, “Due to existence is birth” should be said to him.

“Is there existence due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said to him. If he would say, “Due to what condition is existence?”, “Due to clinging is existence” should be said to him.

“Is there `upādāna` (clinging) due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said to him. If he would say, “Due to what condition is clinging?”, then “Due to the condition of `taṇhā` (craving) is clinging” should be said to him.

“Is there `taṇhā` (craving) due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said. If he would say, “Due to what condition is `taṇhā`?”, thus to him “Due to feeling is `taṇhā`” should be said.

“Is there feeling due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said. If he would say, “Due to what condition is feeling?”, “Due to contact is feeling” should be said to him.

“Is there contact due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said. If he would say, “Due to what condition is contact?”, “Due to `nāmarūpa` (phenomenal objects) is contact” should be said to him.

“Is there `nāmarūpa` (phenomenal objects) due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said to him. If he would say, “Due to what condition is `nāmarūpa`?”, then “Due to consciousness is `nāmarūpa`” should be said to him.

“Is there consciousness due to this?” thus asked, with a virtuous man being, Ānanda, “that there is” should be said. If he would say, “Due to what condition is consciousness?”, “Due to the condition of `nāmarūpa` (phenomenal objects) is consciousness” should be said to him.

97. Thus indeed, Ānanda, dependent on `nāmarūpaṃ` (phenomenal objects) is `viññāṇaṃ` (consciousness), dependent on consciousness is `nāmarūpaṃ`, dependent on `nāmarūpaṃ` is `phasso` (contact), dependent on contact is `vedanā` (feeling), dependent on feeling is `taṇhā` (craving), dependent on craving is `upādānaṃ` (clinging), dependent on clinging is `bhavo` (existence), dependent on existence is `jāti` (birth), dependent on birth `jarāmaraṇaṃ` (old age and death), grief, wailing, pain, displeasure, and despair come to be. Thus is the `samudayo` (origin) of this entire `dukkhakkhandha` (mass of suffering).

98. “That 'due to `jāti` (birth), old age and death' thus indeed this was said; that, Ānanda, should be understood by this way just this, just as due to birth, old age and death. For birth, Ānanda, would not have been by all, all, in every way, all, for anyone, anything, as follows—for devas or for divinity; for `gandhabba` (demigods) or for demigod state; for `yakkha` (spirits) or for daemon state; for beings or for beingness; for humans or for human state; for quadrupeds or for quadruped state; for birds or for bird state; for snakes or for snake state; and of those, for those beings, for that state, birth would not have been. Indeed, if birth entirely eats, with the cessation of birth, would `jarāmaraṇaṃ` (old age and death) then be apparent? No indeed, bhante. Therefore thus, Ānanda, just this is the reason, this is the `nidāna` (source), this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the `paccayo` (condition) of old age and death, namely this, birth.

99. “`Bhavapaccayā` (dependent on existence) `jātī` (birth)’—thus indeed this was said, that, Ānanda, by this also should be understood in this way, as dependent on existence `jāti` (birth). For indeed, Ānanda, if `bhavo` (existence) would not have been at all, completely, in every way, for anyone, in anything, as follows—`kāmabhavo` (existence in the realm of sense desire) or `rūpabhavo` (existence in the realm of form) or `arūpabhavo` (existence in the formless realm), from all existence, when existence eats, from the cessation of existence, would birth also then be apparent?” “No, Bhante.” “Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of birth, namely, existence.

100. “`Upādānapaccayā` (dependent on clinging) `bhavo` (existence)”—thus indeed this was said, that, Ānanda, by this also should be understood in this way, as dependent on clinging, existence. For indeed, Ānanda, if `upādāna` (clinging) would not have been at all, completely, in every way, for anyone, in anything, as follows—`kāmupādānaṃ` (grasping sensual pleasures) or `diṭṭhupādānaṃ` (grasping views) or `sīlabbatupādānaṃ` (grasping precepts and practices) or `attavādupādānaṃ` (grasping a theory of self), in all clinging, when clinging eats, from the cessation of clinging, would existence also then be apparent?” “No, Bhante.” “Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of existence, namely, clinging.

101. “`Taṇhāpaccayā` (dependent on craving) `upādānan` (clinging),” thus indeed this was said, that, Ānanda, by this also should be understood in this way, as dependent on craving `upādānaṃ` (clinging). For indeed, Ānanda, if `taṇhā` (craving) would not have been at all, completely, in every way, for anyone, for anything, as follows—`rūpataṇhā` (craving for material form), `saddataṇhā` (craving for sound), `gandhataṇhā` (craving for smell), `rasataṇhā` (craving for tastes), `phoṭṭhabbataṇhā` (craving for physical sensation), `dhammataṇhā` (craving for mental phenomena)—in all craving, when craving eats, from the cessation of craving, would clinging also then be apparent?” “No, Bhante.” “Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of clinging, namely, craving.

102. “`Vedanāpaccayā` (dependent on feeling) `taṇhā` (craving),” thus indeed this was said, that, Ānanda, by this also should be understood in this way, as dependent on feeling craving. For indeed, Ānanda, if `vedanā` (feeling) would not have been at all, completely, in every way, for anyone, anything—namely: feeling born from eye contact, feeling born from ear contact, feeling born from nose contact, feeling born from tongue contact, feeling born from body contact, feeling born from mind contact; in all feeling, when feeling eats, from the cessation of feeling, would craving also then be apparent?” “No, Bhante.” “Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for craving, namely, feeling.

103. Thus indeed, Ānanda, dependent on `vedanā` (feeling), `taṇhā` (craving); dependent on craving, quest; dependent on quest, gain; dependent on gain, discrimination; dependent on discrimination, interest and passion; dependent on interest and passion, fixation; dependent on fixation, personal possession; dependent on personal possession, selfishness; dependent on selfishness, protection. For the sake of protecting, many bad, `akusalā` (unskilful) Dhammā come to be: taking up sticks and weapons, quarrels disputes and arguments, reciprocal abuse, slander and lies.

104. “For the sake of protecting, taking up sticks and weapons, quarrels disputes and arguments, reciprocal abuse, slander and lies—many bad, `akusalā` (unskilful) Dhammā come to be,” thus indeed this was said, that, Ānanda, by this also should be understood in this way, as for the sake of protecting, taking up sticks and weapons, quarrels disputes and arguments, reciprocal abuse, slander and lies—many bad, unskilful Dhammā come to be. For indeed, Ānanda, if protection would not have been at all, completely, in every way, for anyone, in anything, in all protection, when protection eats, from the cessation of safeguarding, would many bad, unskilful Dhammā such as taking up sticks and weapons, quarrels disputes and arguments, reciprocal abuse, slander and lies also come to be?” “No, Bhante.” “Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for the arising of many bad, unwholesome Dhammā namely: taking up sticks and weapons, quarrels disputes and arguments, reciprocal abuse, slander and lies, namely, protection.

105. “Depending on stinginess, there is protection,” thus indeed this was said, that, Ānanda, by this also should be understood in this way, as depending on stinginess, there is protection. For indeed, Ānanda, if stinginess would not have been at all, in every way, for anyone, in anything; in all stinginess, when stinginess eats, from the cessation of stinginess, would protection also then be apparent? “No, Bhante.” “Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for protection, namely stinginess.

106. “Depending on `pariggahaṃ` (possession), stinginess,” thus indeed this was said. And that, Ānanda, should be understood by this method: as depending on possession, stinginess. For indeed, Ānanda, if there were not at all, in every way, for anyone, anything, possession; when possession is not existing, from the cessation of possession, would also stinginess be apparent? “No, Bhante.” Therefore, Ānanda, this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of stinginess, namely possession.

107. “Dependent on fixation is possession,” thus indeed this was said; that, Ānanda, by this also way should be understood: as dependent on fixation is possession. For indeed, Ānanda, if fixation would not have been entirely, completely, in every way, for anyone, of anything; when fixation is not existing, from the cessation of fixation, would also possession be apparent? “No, Bhante.” Therefore, Ānanda, this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of possession—namely, this fixation.

108. “Depending on interest and passion, there is fixation,” thus indeed this was said. And that, Ānanda, should be understood by this method as well: just as depending on interest and passion, there is fixation. For indeed, Ānanda, if interest and passion would not have been entirely, completely, in every way, for anyone, in anything; if interest and passion is not existing, from the cessation of interest and passion, would also fixation then be apparent? “No, Bhante.” Therefore, Ānanda, this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for fixation, namely interest and passion.

109. “Depending on opinion, desire and lust,” thus indeed this was said. That, Ānanda, should be understood by this way also, just as desire and lust depends on opinion. For indeed, Ānanda, if opinion would not have been entirely, in every way, for anyone, in anything; if opinion is not existing, from the cessation of opinion, would also desire and lust then be apparent? “No, Bhante.” Therefore, Ānanda, this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of desire and lust, namely, opinion.

110. “Depending on gain, there is evaluation,” thus indeed this was said. And that, Ānanda, should be understood by this way also: as depending on gain, there is evaluation. For indeed, Ānanda, if gain would not have been at all, completely, in every way, for anyone, in anything; when gain is not existing, from the cessation of gain, would also evaluation then be apparent? “No, Bhante.” Therefore, Ānanda, this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of evaluation: namely, gain.

111. “Gain depends on seeking,” thus indeed this was said. And, Ānanda, this is to be understood by this other way as well: just as gain depends on seeking. For, indeed, Ānanda, if there were no seeking at all, in every way, for anyone, in anything, when seeking is not existing, from the cessation of seeking, would also gain then be apparent? “No, Bhante.” Therefore, Ānanda, this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition of gain, namely seeking.

112. “`taṇhā` (Craving) as condition, quest” thus indeed this was said; that, Ānanda, by this also way should be understood: as craving as condition, quest. And indeed, Ānanda, if craving would not have been, for anyone, in anything, entirely, completely, in every way—namely: craving for sensual pleasure, craving for becoming, craving for nonexistence—when craving is not existing, from the cessation of craving, would also quest be apparent? No, Bhante. Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the reason, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for seeking, namely, craving. Thus indeed, Ānanda, these two `dhammā` (teachings) become united in one flow by `vedanā` (feeling) by duality.

113. “feeling dependent on contact”—thus indeed this was said, O Ānanda; and this should be understood by this way also, just as feeling dependent on contact. For indeed, O Ānanda, if `phasso` (contact) were not at all, in every way, for anyone, of anything—namely: eye-contact, ear-contact, nose-contact, tongue-contact, body-contact, mind-contact—when all contact is not existing, due to the cessation of contact, would `vedanā` (feeling) be apparent?” “No, Bhante.” “Therefore, O Ānanda, this is the cause, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for feeling, namely: contact.

114. “contact dependent on name and form,” thus indeed this was said; that, Ānanda, should be understood by this way also, as contact dependent on name and form. By these, Ānanda, by these ways, by these characteristics, by these signs, by these propositions, the designation of the name group is; in those ways, in those characteristics, in those signs, in those propositions, even when not existing, surely would the nameable impression of the material body be apparent? No, Bhante. “By which, Ānanda, ways, by which characteristics, by which signs, by which propositions the designation of the material body is, in those ways…pe…in those propositions, even when not existing, would the contact of impact in the name body be apparent?” No, Bhante. “By these, Ānanda, ways…pe… by these propositions, the designation of the name group and the material body is; in those ways…pe… in those propositions, even when not existing, would nameable impression or physical impression be apparent?” No, Bhante. “By these, Ānanda, ways…pe… by these propositions the designation of `nāmarūpa` (phenomenal objects) is, in those ways…pe… in those propositions even if not existing, would `phasso` (contact) be apparent?” No, Bhante. Therefore, Ānanda, this very is the reason, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for contact, namely, phenomenal objects.

115. “name and form dependent on consciousness,” thus indeed this was said; that, Ānanda, should be understood by this way also, as name and form dependent on consciousness. And indeed, Ānanda, if `viññāṇa` (consciousness) had not entered the mother's womb, would `nāmarūpa` (phenomenal objects) indeed have come into being in the mother's womb? No, Bhante. “Consciousness, indeed, having entered the mother’s womb, would you have miscarried, or would phenomenal objects have come into existence for such an existence?” No, Bhante. And indeed, Ānanda, if consciousness were completely cut off from a young boy or a young girl, would phenomenal objects then come to growth, increase, and Vepulla? No, Bhante. Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the reason, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for phenomenal objects—namely, consciousness.

116. “consciousness dependent on name and form,” thus indeed this was said; that, Ānanda, should be understood by this way also, as consciousness dependent on name and form. And indeed, Ānanda, if you would not obtain `viññāṇa` (consciousness) as a support in `nāmarūpa` (phenomenal objects), would the generation of the arising of suffering, birth, old age and death, be evident in the future? No, Bhante. Therefore, Ānanda, just this is the reason, this is the origin, this is the `samudayo` (origin), this is the condition for consciousness, namely, phenomenal objects. To this extent indeed, Ānanda, one oneself could be born or diminishes or dies or passes away or is reborn. To this extent the pathway of expression, to this extent the way of speech, to this extent the pathway of description, to this extent the extent of wisdom, to this extent the circle proceeds for the designating of the present state, namely phenomenal objects with consciousness in the state of interdependence moves forward.
