# Prajñāpāramitāstotra (Hymn to the Perfection of Wisdom)

## Rāhulabhadra: Prajñāpāramitāstotra

`nirvikalpe` (indivisible one), homage to you, `prajñāpāramite` (perfection of wisdom), `amite` (incomparable one)  
which you, `sarvānavadyāṅgi` (one whose limbs are all faultless), will examine with the faultless.

Like space, without attachment, without proliferation, without letters  
whoever sees you by conception, he sees the Tathāgata.

Of you, O teacher, and of the Buddha, the world-teacher  
beings do not see an interval, like between the moon and its rays.

Having compassion as their nature, taking refuge in you, the foremost of the Buddha's Dharma  
they go happily to the incomparable greatness, `bhaktivatsale` (one who is compassionate).

Even once, in a pure aspiration, whoever contemplates you properly  
by that too, certainly, success is attained, `amoghadarśane` (one of infallible vision).

Of all even heroes whose selves are devoted to the welfare of others  
you are the nourisher and mother, compassionate.

Since the Buddhas, world-teachers, are your sons, compassionate  
therefore you too, `kalyāṇi` (auspicious one), are the great-grandmother of all beings.

By all perfections, you, pure, `anindite` (blameless one)  
like a moonbeam, you are followed always by stars.

Having reached the people to be trained there and there by Tathāgatas  
you alone indeed, of many forms, are praised by various names.

Like having received light, drops of water in the sun  
having reached you, faults and disputes of disputants go to dissolution.

You indeed are the generator of fear for the foolish, of terrifying vision  
and you are the generator of reassurance for the wise, of gentle vision.

In whom even the clinging to that nātha does not exist  
of him, amba, how will passion and hatred exist elsewhere.

You do not come from anywhere, nor do you go anywhere  
in places too, and in all, by the wise you are not apprehended.

Those who do not see you thus, yet take refuge in existence  
and taking refuge, they are released; this is a great wonder.

You indeed are bound by one who sees, and even by one who does not see  
you indeed are released by one who sees, and even by one who does not see.

Oh, you are astonishing, you are profound, `yaśasvini` (glorious one)  
you are hard to understand; like illusion you are seen, yet you are not seen.

By Buddhas, pratyekabuddhas, and śrāvakas, it is prohibited  
you are the path; for release there is no other, this is certain.

Placing convention first, for the sake of designation of embodied beings  
out of compassion by lokanāthas you are spoken of, and yet not spoken of.

Who is able here to praise you, without sign, without stain  
beyond all verbal objects, in whom you are unattached anywhere.

Even though it is true, by convention, by such paths of speech we  
taking refuge in you, even while praising, are satisfied, being well content.

Having praised the perfection of wisdom, which auspicious thing I have composed  
by that may the entire world quickly become devoted to the perfection of wisdom.
