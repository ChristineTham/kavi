# Prajñāpāramitāstotra (Hymn to the Perfection of Wisdom)

## Rāhulabhadra: Prajñāpāramitāstotra

`nirvikalpe` (without change) salutations are to you, `prajñāpāramite` (perfection of wisdom), `amite` (immeasurable),  
you who are observed by the faultless ones as `sarvānavadyāṅgi` (all-faultless-limbed),

Like space, stainless, free from proliferation, and without letters,  
he who observes you by existence, him indeed observes the Tathāgata.

Of your teacher, the one possessing many qualities, and of the Buddha, the world-teacher,  
the peaceful ones do not see an internal difference, like the moon and the moonlight.

Through compassion, they take refuge in you, the shore of the Buddha-dharma,  
they come with happiness, `bhaktivatsale` (devotion-cherishing one), of incomparable greatness.

Even if once, in a pure intention, he who observes you according to the rule,  
by him also certain success is attained, `amoghadarśane` (unfailing-visioned one).

Among all the heroes, those devoted to the sake of others,  
you are the nurturer and the creator, a mother, `vatsalā` (affectionate one).

Because the Buddhas, the world-teachers, are your sons through compassion,  
therefore you also, `kalyāṇi` (auspicious one), are the grandfather of all beings.

Through all perfections, you, `anindite` (unblamable one), among the pure ones,  
are always followed by the moon-rays like the moon-rays,

Having brought the people to discipline, there in various places by the Tathāgatas,  
you alone, `kalyāṇi` (auspicious one), are praised by many.

Having attained the light, the water-drops of the shining ones,  
having attained you, the faults and the arguments of the disputants go to destruction.

You alone are the mother of those in fear, `bhīmadarśanā` (terrible-visioned one),  
and you are the mother of those in confidence, `saumyadarśanā` (gentle-visioned one).

Because of whom there is attachment, even though your nātha is not found,  
`amba` (mother), how elsewhere will passion and hatred exist?

You go nowhere, from anywhere, and you go nowhere at all,  
in all places, you are not perceived by the wise.

Those who do not observe you thus, and who take refuge through existence,  
they take refuge and are released; this is great and wonderful.

You alone are bound, he who observes also is bound,  
you alone are released, he who observes also is released.

Ah, you are astonishing, you are profound, `yaśasvini` (illustrious one),  
you are difficult to understand, like a dream, you are seen and yet you are not seen.

Served by Buddhas, pratyekabuddhas, and śrāvakas,  
you alone are the path of mokṣa; there is no other, this is the certainty.

Taking convention as foremost, for the sake of designation of the embodied ones,  
through compassion, by the lokanāthas, you are spoken of and yet not spoken of.

Who is able here to praise you, the uncaused and stainless,  
surpassing all speech, you who are anywhere un-reliant.

Truly, even so, through convention and by such words of speech as these,  
while being in praise of you, the satisfied ones are well-restrained.

Having praised the Prajñāpāramitā, the good which was performed by me,  
may that quickly turn the whole world toward the perfection of wisdom.
