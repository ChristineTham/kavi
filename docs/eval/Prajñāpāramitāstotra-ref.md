# Prajñāpāramitāstotra (Hymn to the Perfection of Wisdom)

## Rāhulabhadra: Prajñāpāramitāstotra

Homage to you, `nirvikalpe` (free of conceptualisation) `prajñāpāramitā` (perfection of wisdom), `amite` (immeasurable),  
you who, `sarvānavadyāṅgi` (irreproachable in every limb), are beheld by the irreproachable.

Whoever sees you, unstained like space, free of `prapañca` (proliferation), without letters,  
with the heart, he sees the Tathāgata.

And between you, ārye, rich in good qualities, and the Buddha, the teacher of the world,  
the good see no difference, as between the moon and moonlight.

Those whose nature is compassion, having resorted to you, who go before the dharmas of the Buddha,  
easily come to unequalled greatness, `bhaktivatsale` (tender to the devoted).

Whoever, the intention being pure, beholds you duly even once,  
by him too success is certainly attained, `amoghadarśane` (whose sight is not in vain).

Of all the heroes too, whose selves delight in the welfare of others,  
you are the nourisher and the begetter, the tender mother.

Since the Buddhas, the teachers of the world, the compassionate, are your sons,  
therefore you too, `kalyāṇi` (good one), are the grandmother of all beings.

By all the stainless perfections, `anindite` (blameless one), you  
are always followed, as the crescent moon by the stars.

By the Tathāgatas, having reached the people to be trained here and there,  
you, just the one of many forms, are praised under various names.

As dewdrops on reaching the radiance of the blazing-rayed sun,  
so on reaching you the faults and the doctrines of the disputants go to dissolution.

You indeed, fearsome to behold, are the producer of terror in the foolish,  
and, gentle to behold, you are the producer of reassurance in the wise.

He who, having you as nātha, has no affection even for you,  
how, amba, will passion and hatred arise for him towards anything else?

You do not come from anywhere, and you do not go anywhere,  
and in all places, too, you are not apprehended by the wise.

Those who do not see you thus, and resort to you from the heart,  
and having resorted are released: this is a great wonder.

Seeing you indeed, one is bound; not seeing, too, one is bound.  
Seeing you indeed, one is released; not seeing, too, one is released.

Ah, you are astonishing, you are profound, `yaśasvini` (glorious one),  
you are very hard to understand; like an illusion you are seen and you are not seen.

Resorted to by the Buddhas, the pratyekabuddhas and the śrāvakas,  
you are the one path to `mokṣa` (release); there is no other: this is certain.

Putting `vyavahāra` (convention) first, for the sake of `prajñapti` (designation) for the embodied,  
out of compassion you are spoken of by the lokanāthas, and you are not spoken of.

Who here is able to praise you, signless, spotless,  
gone beyond the whole range of speech, you who are dependent on nothing anywhere?

Even this being so, by `saṃvṛti` (convention), with such ways of speech, we,  
wishing to praise you though you are not to be praised, are fully at ease.

Whatever good has been accumulated by me through praising the prajñāpāramitā,  
by that may the whole world quickly become intent on the far shore of wisdom.
